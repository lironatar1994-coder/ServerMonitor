# Backend

## Purpose

- Provide the Express API served under `/serve-monitor/api`.
- Own the SQLite monitor database, authentication, app CRUD/status endpoints, PM2 integration, system stats, logs, and background monitoring.

## Ownership

- `pageInsights.js` owns exact-page anonymous visit cohorts, ordered next-page transitions and subsequent observed actions. `trackingHealth.js` reads the bounded browser-check result for authenticated dashboards.

- `growthSchema.js` owns idempotent growth tables and canonical client defaults. `clientGrowth.js` and `routes/clientGrowth.js` own authenticated internal goals, workflow, evidence, campaigns, comparisons and draft reports. `growthSignals.js` validates anonymous action beacons; `growthSources.js` polls allow-listed source stores read-only once per minute.

- `server.js` owns middleware, API mounting, frontend static serving, and process startup.
- `database.js` owns schema creation, migrations, explicit bootstrap accounts, and seed monitor records.
- `monitor.js` owns background health, PM2, log, metric, and alert collection.
- `httpHealth.js` owns fresh-connection HTTP probes with a five-second total deadline per attempt (ten for public URL probes), one transport-failure retry, and error codes for failed checks. HTTP failures remain visible immediately; recovered latency includes the failed attempt.
- `logParser.js` owns host-aware Nginx access-log filtering, visitor parsing, and heuristic bot classification.
- `visitorAnalytics.js` owns cursor-based access-log ingestion, initial bounded backfill, GeoIP enrichment, and raw-event retention.
- `browserSignals.js` owns authenticated first-party browser-signal site matching, validation, identifier hashing, path/IP normalization, bot filtering, deduplication, and persistence for navigation, engagement, heatmap, and product-use events.
- `resourceUsage.js` owns complete PM2 process-tree attribution and cached, bounded production-storage scans.
- `alerts.js` owns alert rendering (down, slow, silent, recovered) and delivery by Resend email plus `push.js` browser push. `monitor.js` lists PM2 once per cycle, times the health URL and public URL, batches one alert per cycle, alerts after two consecutive failed cycles, repeats every six hours, marks `last_alerted_at` (or `slow-alert:`/`silent-alert:` metadata) only after a delivery succeeds and reports recovery only for reported failures.
- `observability.js` owns `status_events` (availability), `metrics.response_ms`, `deploy_events` (runtime starts from PM2 `pm_uptime` and systemd `ActiveEnterTimestamp`), `chart_notes`, `web_vitals` (p75, 90-day retention) and real-visitor 404 and silent-tracker queries. `idx_metrics_app_time` keeps per-app metric reads indexed.
- `operationsViews.js` reads backup manifests, PC acknowledgements, daily status, CRM backups and the Alenu CRM export (names, sizes and dates only) and checks TLS certificates of catalog hosts, cached for six hours.
- `sources.js` maps campaign tags and referring hosts to channels and summarises growth sessions per channel, campaign and referring site. Tracked links (`tracked_links`, unique per host and code) redirect `https://<site>/l/<code>` through the public `routes/go.js` to the site with `utm_source=<channel>&utm_medium=link&utm_campaign=<campaign or code>`; only the stored site host is ever a destination, unknown codes go to the site home, and `link_clicks` keep a timestamp and automation flag only (internal browsers are not recorded).
- `insights.js` runs every 30 minutes from the monitor cycle, detects insights with stable keys in `insight_notifications` (90-day retention) and pushes each once. Thresholds: traffic spikes need a 5/day baseline and 30+ estimated visits at twice the usual; drops need a 30/day baseline and under 40%; contact clicks need 5+ at twice the usual; new pages need 20+ views and none in the prior week; 404s need 10+ visitors; slower pages need 20+ samples in both periods. Inquiry notices start from the first run, never replaying history.
- `push.js` and `routes/push.js` store per-user Web Push subscriptions with per-device categories, quiet hours (Israel time) and a daily insight cap, logging deliveries in `push_deliveries` (30 days); VAPID keys live in `PUSH_KEY_FILE` (production `/root/.server-monitor-push-key.json`, mode 0600), never in the database. Monitor sessions cannot subscribe.
- `catalogNames.js` names the catalog records the monitor treats specially (`תחזוקה לילית`, `אבטחת SSH`); renames go through `renamedProductionApps` so IDs and history stay.
- `emailReports.js` owns daily and weekly client comparison reports, Resend delivery, scheduling, and delivery deduplication.
- `emailReportTemplate.js` owns shared Hebrew HTML/plain-text report presentation and app-specific monitor links.
- `remoteControl.js` owns the Claude Remote relay for several PCs, each named by its agent's `host`: per-PC sessions, links, status and last screenshot, an in-memory job queue whose jobs each target one PC, and per-PC long-polls. Session lists persist in `RC_STATE_FILE` (default `backend/.remote-state.json`); PCs unseen for 30 days are dropped, at most 10 are kept. `routes/remote.js` exposes `/remote/agent/*` to the PC agents and owner-only `/remote/state?pc=`, `/remote/jobs` (`pc` in the body) and `/remote/screenshot?pc=`; without `pc` the most recently seen PC is used.
- `externalChecks.js` stores only the latest outside result per site and source (`external_checks`) with consecutive failures; `/remote/agent/probe-targets` and `/remote/agent/probes` serve the PC agents. `outsideOnlyFailures` feeds the monitor cycle's `external` alert (server online, two fresh outside failures, six-hour reminder). Runs with most targets failing are stored as inconclusive in `monitor_metadata` (`external-run:<source>`) and change no site.
- Each completed monitor cycle stores `monitor-cycle-at` in `monitor_metadata`; `/remote/agent/probe-targets` returns it as `cycle_at` for the PC agents' watchdog. The nightly-maintenance record's `down` alert carries up to three host-check errors as `detail`, rendered in Hebrew by `alerts.js` for known messages.
- `/apps/server-stats` includes `updates` (pending and security package counts) read from `/var/lib/lawebs-maintenance/updates.json`; `operationalHealth` raises `עדכוני אבטחה לא הותקנו` when maintenance reports security updates pending over two days.
- `capacity.js` samples root-disk and memory once an hour from `statfs` and `/proc/meminfo` inside the monitor cycle (`host_samples`, 120 days) and serves a 30-minute cached forecast in `/apps/server-stats` (`forecast.disk`: days to full from a two-week least-squares slope, needing two days of samples; `forecast.memory_growth`: up to three services that grew over 30% and 50 MB from a week ago).
- `jsErrors.js` folds visitor script errors (signal kind `error`) into one `js_errors` row per site, day, page and signature, at most 200 rows per site per day, 30-day retention. Messages lose URLs, e-mail addresses and 4+ digit numbers; sources keep only the file name; `Script error.` and automation are dropped. `/visitor-analytics/apps/:id/js-errors` returns the top signatures.
- `routes/` owns HTTP route handlers and request/response contracts.
- `monitor.db` is runtime state and must not be treated as a source schema definition.

## Local Contracts

- `operationalHealth.js` and authenticated GET `/apps/operational-health` expose a sanitized read-only attention view: recent host/browser/backup snapshots, report timestamps, active custom systemd apps missing from the catalog, legacy alert-queue counts and Seder connection readiness. This view does not create catalog records, change backup policy, send messages or restart services.
- Read bounded JSON files (64 KiB), query the legacy message store read-only for aggregate pending counts only, and never expose recipients, message contents, QR codes or pairing data. Custom-unit discovery is limited to 100 names in `/etc/systemd/system`, cached 60 seconds and bounded to three seconds; unavailable discovery is explicit.
- Operational health warns when the newest PC backup acknowledgement is older than 48 hours or missing; an unreadable acknowledgement folder is not reported as a gap.
- Operational source freshness is 30 minutes for host health, seven hours for browser checks, 36 hours for documented backups and three minutes for messaging readiness. Keep failures and missing sources visible; prioritize delivery/connection/coverage warnings ahead of routine maintenance. Source-specific checks passing never imply complete catalog or backup coverage.


- `security.js`, `routes/sessions.js` and the `routes/auth.js` compatibility export own hashed opaque sessions, Argon2id login hashes, legacy bcrypt migration, mandatory TOTP and hashed one-use recovery codes. No default password or JWT fallback. Remembered devices (`auth_devices`) are created only by `/auth/mfa/verify` with `remember: true`; the `__Host-monitor-device` cookie (HttpOnly, Secure, SameSite=Strict, 30 days absolute) holds a random token stored only as a SHA-256 hash, rotated on every resume through GET `/auth/session` when no valid session exists. A previous token is honoured for 60 seconds (parallel tabs); later reuse revokes that device and its sessions. Resume refuses cross-site `Sec-Fetch-Site`, disabled users, missing MFA and pending password upgrades, and keeps the device's original code time in `mfa_at` so `recentMfa` still asks again. At most 10 devices per user; only a `browser · system` label is stored. Logout forgets the current device; password change and session revocation remove devices; `/auth/devices` lists and deletes them. Legacy login passwords shorter than 15 characters must be changed through a password-verified pending session before MFA; the update revokes other sessions. Bootstrap only through an explicit `MONITOR_BOOTSTRAP_PASSWORD` of at least 15 characters.
- Production uses `NODE_ENV=production`, exact `MONITOR_ORIGIN=https://monitor.vee-app.co.il`, a Secure/HttpOnly/SameSite=Strict host-only cookie and CSRF+Origin checks on mutations. Auth/vault APIs reject alias hosts; SPA aliases redirect to the canonical origin. Bind the API to loopback.
- `AUTH_KEY_FILE` is a persistent root-only 32-byte key for TOTP secrets, never the vault encryption key. Never log auth/vault URLs, request bodies, tokens, passphrases or decrypted records.
- Pending MFA setup reuses the encrypted pending seed across refresh/retry so an already-scanned Google Authenticator account remains valid. Return its standard six-digit/30-second TOTP URI only to the password-verified pending user; completed enrollment cannot retrieve or replace its existing seed through setup.
- Monitoring automation receives a five-minute read-only `monitor` session. It cannot enter any vault route, mutate records, read logs or access message-worker data. Human bearer tokens are rejected. Owner checks protect infrastructure/service actions and client-workspace mutations.
- `vault.js` owns the shared-vault schema and opaque envelopes; `routes/vault.js` is feature-gated by `VAULT_ENABLED=true`. Metadata and secrets are encrypted separately in the browser; the server sees opaque IDs, versions, epochs, account roles and audit event types only. Browser encryption protects stored blobs; delivered JavaScript and the unlocked device remain trust boundaries. Do not claim protection against a compromised application server or a completed independent security audit.
- Grant access only after owner verification of the member public-key fingerprint. Invites are hashed, single-use, expire after 24 hours and are manually copied. MFA recency is five minutes for key/member/invite/rotation/permanent-delete changes. Prevent removal of the last active owner with a vault key.
- Entry writes use optimistic content versions; rotation validates the full entry/member set and global revision in one SQLite transaction. Revocation deletes sessions/member envelopes and freezes writes until rotation. Keep a 1,000-entry/8 MiB ciphertext quota, 128 KiB ordinary requests and 16 MiB authenticated rotation requests.
- Trash remains recoverable for 30 days; purge on vault requests, at most hourly. Audit contains event type, account ID, opaque item ID and timestamp, retained 90 days; a secret fetch is not proof of copy or reveal.
- Remote control is owner-only for human sessions (never the read-only monitor session). The agent authenticates only with a bearer token compared against the SHA-256 lines in `RC_AGENT_TOKEN_FILE` (production `/root/.server-monitor-rc-agent.sha256`, one per installed agent, at most five, re-read on change); human cookies never authenticate the agent and the agent token never reaches panel routes. Agent routes mount before the global JSON parser (512 KB reports, 3 MB JPEG/PNG screenshots) and are kept out of request logs. Job types are an allow-list (`list`, `connect`, `disconnect`, `status`, `screenshot`, `launch`); queued jobs expire after 90 seconds and running jobs after 150. Each PC holds at most one pending poll; a new poll replaces only that PC's own. A job reaches only its target PC; reports and screenshots carry no host and are attributed through the job they answer, so a screenshot without a matching screenshot job is refused. Only `https://claude.ai/` links are stored; collapsed sidebar groups lose their `Show N more in` prefix. Alias hosts may reach only `/remote/agent/`.
- The app invokes the existing system PM2 executable; keep the unused npm `pm2` dependency absent.


- Cross-site new/returning lookups use partial candidate IP/time indexes and a global time index; keep the 1/30/90-day overview under the browser-check latency budget. Dashboard HTML is not cacheable and missing asset chunks return 404.
- Browser-check results become stale after seven hours, allowing the six-hour production cadence plus scheduling grace; failed results remain visible immediately.
- Portfolio action metadata accepts only known project slugs and placement names. LA webs engagement batches contain time deltas; aggregate their time per session/page and use maximum scroll reach. Other sites retain their existing measurement semantics.
- Page insights scope every query by app, selected period and exact page. Derive next steps from ordered non-automated growth page events, never by joining IPs to contacts. Fewer than 30 measured visits is a low sample; contacts remain observations, not leads or causal evidence.
- Lead states include `contacted` and `qualified`; both remain open for follow-up. An outcome is still manual or source-confirmed independently of browser actions.

- Growth routes must never accept the Manager Site service key or expose customer-facing access. Scope every record mutation to the canonical app and record ID; validate metric/status/path/number fields and keep an activity trail.
- Growth source rows retain only opaque/source IDs, timestamps and statuses. Exclude known deployment fixtures, deduplicate `(app, origin, external_key)`, preserve manual treatment on sync, and expose failed/stale adapters without clearing prior data. Native source status is distinct from internal workflow.
- Dfus Reuven keeps legacy `quotes` history and imports new `store.json` orders/contacts under separate origins. Nested customer fixture markers are checked before discarding private fields. Aggregate all adapters per app: any failed source keeps the app source state failed and preserves its last complete success, even when another source imports successfully. Store `completed` means handled, never paid/revenue; `in_review`/`ready` mean working until an internal edit overrides them.
- Source-approved/closed registrations become `completed` (handled in source), never revenue or won deals. Source workflow updates apply only until the first internal lead edit; cancelled rows are irrelevant. Newly identified test fixtures are archived from business totals without deleting source records. Registration adapters treat a whole event as a QA fixture when a fifth or more of its registrations carry test markers, or when the event was inserted in a same-second batch of three or more events and has a test-marked registration.
- Growth events have a 90-day retention, site-scoped session hashes, no form values/query strings, explicit automation filtering, a bounded per-site/IP rate limit, and browser types cannot create confirmed leads. Source-confirmed leads cannot be attributed to browser sessions without a real join.
- Goals define their own 7/30/90-day period. Before/after task comparisons use equal full-day windows up to seven days and label low samples and confounders. Do not infer causality or call click counts leads. Campaign budgets are lifetime amounts; never divide them by leads from a partial selected period.
